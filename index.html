BACKGROUND / ABOUT ME

An engineering journey.
A practical perspective.

I am Janaka Madhusankha, a Ph.D. candidate at Southern Illinois University Carbondale, focusing on control and optimization.

My research develops control algorithms for an advanced electromechanical system that uses an array of permanent magnets to precisely manipulate magnetic objects within a dedicated region. This work has implications for biomedical applications, including targeted drug delivery, remote catheter guidance, and minimally invasive surgical procedures.

I hold a bachelor’s degree in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka. This provided a strong foundation in both electrical and electronic engineering.

My experience at Rinstrum honed my skills in embedded systems and hardware design and gave me valuable insight into industrial project management. Across research and product development, I enjoy connecting a sound model with a system that works in practice.

I have also supported laboratory teaching at SIU Carbondale, helping students connect engineering principles with practical experiments.

03 / RESEARCH · SIU CARBONDALE

Precision motion.
Zero mechanical contact.

An integrated experimental platform: magnetic field modeling, mechanical design, high-speed vision, feedback control, and servomotor actuation.

Why permanent magnets?

Magnetic fields can guide magnetic objects without mechanical contact. This has implications for targeted drug delivery, remote catheter guidance, minimally invasive procedures, and milli- and microscale manipulation.

Our research investigates permanent magnets as compact, economically viable alternatives to electromagnet-based manipulators, particularly where strong magnetic fields are needed. This project is supported by the National Science Foundation.

The experimental system

A soft magnetic bead moves in a Petri dish containing corn syrup. Six diametrically magnetized permanent magnets surround the planar workspace. Each is independently rotated by a Dynamixel AX-18A servomotor.

An Allied Vision Mako U-130B high-speed camera measures bead position. The setup was designed in SolidWorks and fabricated using 3D printing.

01 / SOFTWARE

Vision to actuation

Two custom LabVIEW programs handle Gauss-sensor calibration and feedback control. An integrated GUI supports controller tuning and data visualization. The vision algorithm measures bead position, and a serial link updates servomotor setpoints.

02 / MODELING

Field model to feedback

We developed an analytical magnetic field model for rapid workspace calculations. COMSOL Multiphysics finite element analysis determined critical model parameters, supporting faster control calculations and precise bead manipulation.

EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS

Watch the controller at work.

ELECTRIC VEHICLES / MODELING & CONTROL

Two energy sources.
One sharing strategy.

My undergraduate project paired lead-acid batteries with a supercapacitor in a hybrid energy storage system for an electric three-wheeler. The goal was to extend battery life by sharing the load with a secondary energy source.

My contribution In our three-student group, I designed the load-sharing algorithm and proposed a Kalman-filter-based power-sharing approach to reduce battery degradation.

We presented a results poster at the University of Peradeniya’s 2017 engineering research symposium and published our findings at the Institution of Engineers, Sri Lanka’s annual sessions that year.
